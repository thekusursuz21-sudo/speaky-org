Turkish Language Exchange Online

Real conversations can turn vocabulary into usable language when both partners get a fair chance to practise.

What is a language exchange?

A language exchange pairs people who want to practise each other's languages. Instead of a formal teacher-student relationship, both participants contribute. For example, an English speaker learning Turkish might spend part of a conversation in Turkish and another part helping a Turkish speaker practise English.

Agree on a simple structure

Decide roughly how much time to spend in each language and whether you want corrections immediately or after you finish a thought. Constant correction can interrupt fluency, while no correction at all may allow repeated mistakes to become habits.

Use topics, not vocabulary lists

Choose a subject such as food, travel, work, hobbies or a recent film. Try to explain opinions and experiences. When you discover a word you do not know, learn it because you needed it in a real conversation. That context makes vocabulary easier to remember.

Text chat has advantages

Written conversation gives learners time to notice spelling and Turkish-specific characters. It also creates a record of useful phrases you can review. As confidence grows, voice conversation can add pronunciation and listening practice.

Keep the exchange balanced

Do not expect the other person to become an unpaid tutor. Both participants should get meaningful practice. If you mainly want structured instruction, a professional course may be a better fit.
