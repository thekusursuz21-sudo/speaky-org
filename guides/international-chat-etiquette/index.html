International Chat Etiquette

Good conversations across cultures begin with curiosity, patience and respect.

Talking with someone from another country can be one of the most interesting parts of an online community. You may discover different food, music, traditions, humour and everyday experiences. Cultural differences can also create misunderstandings, especially when people are communicating quickly or in a language that is not their first.

Start With Curiosity, Not Assumptions

Ask people about their experiences rather than assuming that everyone from a country thinks or behaves the same way. Questions such as “What do you like most about living there?” are usually more welcoming than broad claims about a nationality or culture.

Remember That Tone Does Not Always Translate

Jokes, sarcasm and slang can be difficult to understand across languages. A message that sounds playful to you may sound rude to someone else. If a conversation becomes confusing, explain what you meant instead of escalating the misunderstanding.

Be Patient With Language Differences

Grammar mistakes and unusual wording do not tell you how intelligent or interesting someone is. Many people in international communities communicate in a second or third language. Clear sentences and ordinary vocabulary often make conversations easier for everyone.

Avoid Turning People Into Spokespeople

One person cannot represent an entire country, religion, city or culture. Political and social topics can be interesting, but allow people to speak for themselves rather than expecting them to explain or defend millions of other people.

Find Common Ground

Music, films, sport, travel, food, hobbies and everyday life are useful starting points. Our guide to starting conversations online includes practical examples for opening a discussion naturally.

Respect Boundaries and Privacy

Do not pressure someone for their full name, exact address, workplace, private photographs or contact information. Trust develops gradually. Review our online chat safety guide before sharing personal information with someone you have only recently met.
