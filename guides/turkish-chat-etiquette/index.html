Turkish Chat Etiquette

Good online conversation depends less on memorising rules and more on treating the person behind the screen with respect.

There is no single Turkish personality

Turkey includes many regions, lifestyles and communities. Turkish speakers abroad add even more variety. Cultural context can help you communicate, but stereotypes are a poor substitute for listening to the individual you are talking to.

Greetings and tone

A simple “Merhaba” is a safe opening. Online conversations among friends can become informal quickly, while professional or unfamiliar settings may stay more polite. If you are unsure, begin respectfully and let the other person's tone guide you.

Humour and misunderstandings

Jokes, sarcasm and slang do not always travel well between languages. If a message seems unexpectedly rude, consider whether tone has been lost before assuming bad intent. Likewise, avoid using slang you do not understand merely because you have seen other people use it.

Respect privacy and boundaries

Do not push someone for a photograph, phone number, exact location or social account. A person declining a question is not an invitation to ask again. Avoid turning a friendly conversation into flirting unless the interest is clearly mutual.

Politics, religion and identity

These can produce interesting conversations, but they can also be personal and contentious. Ask rather than assume, disagree with ideas without attacking people, and recognise when someone would rather change the subject.

Learn the language gradually

Safety still matters

Cultural friendliness should never be confused with a reason to ignore normal online precautions. Never send passwords, banking credentials or verification codes. See the chat scam guide if a new contact asks for money or makes unusual claims.
